## Sources

### 1. Behold-Your-God.pdf
**ID:** `81def734-95f7-4ae8-be0a-4974d4fd4a9f` | Type: PDF
The full manuscript. Chapters 15 ("The Supreme Revelation") and 20 ("The Mystery-Unfolding Cross") are the core cross chapters. Ch. 21 covers "The Way of the Cross" — two antagonistic crosses (God's love vs. Satan's coercion). The book's thesis: the mystery of the cross explains all other mysteries; God's attributes (mercy, love, holiness, justice) are seen as beautiful and unified only in the light of Calvary.

---

### 2. Behold Your God: A Synthesis of the Great Controversy
**ID:** `c905f935-bd88-44e5-9322-5b8ce837b16c` | Type: pasted_text
High-level synthesis of the book's argument in block form. The cross is the "pivotal event that unveils God's character" and "the supreme revelation that interprets all apparent contradictions." Core thesis in one sentence: God defeats evil by revealing His true character of love — supremely at the cross, experientially in His people, finally in the vindication of His government before the universe.

---

### 3. Behold Your God: A Thematic Outline
**ID:** `2db7c779-0469-45ca-acd4-de29f85d1c60` | Type: pasted_text
Chapter-by-chapter thematic outline. Ch. 15 = "The Supreme Revelation" (cross demonstrates justice and love united). Ch. 21 = "The Way of the Cross" (cross as infinite revelation of God's love; two crosses — love vs. coercion). Useful for structure and argument flow across chapters.

---

### 4. Christ's Ministry, Law, and the Cross
**ID:** `df64a201-68c9-443a-a539-b531ee38faac` | Type: pasted_text
Argument outlines for the Christ's ministry block (Chs. 16–20). Ch. 19: mystery of iniquity — sin masquerades as righteousness, religious systems mirror Satan's spirit of force. Ch. 20: the cross unveils both the depth of sin and the true nature of God's response. How Christ died: bearing sin's results (separation), not divine infliction. The cross unmasks Satan as destroyer and reveals God as self-giving love.

---

### 5. Divine Character, Prophecy, and Law
**ID:** `3339e286-cb46-424a-a869-7982af61f510` | Type: pasted_text
Covers Chs. 6–10. God's government and law are built on freedom and self-giving love, not coercion. Isaiah's prophecy: Christ's mission was to reveal God's character, not to force. The law is a transcript of God's character — immutable, covering all relationships. Ch. 10: Satan attacks God's character by portraying it as harsh or arbitrary; God allows His principles to be tested in history so their truth can be seen.

---

### 6. Hermeneutics: God's Character and Biblical Interpretation
**ID:** `d0b9d924-6ad4-4f59-adc2-ad369fdbd7d0` | Type: pasted_text
Argument outlines for the interpretation block (Chs. 11–15). The cross is the "decisive revelation of how God deals with evil and sin" and must be the central lens for reading all Scripture. When the Bible says "God destroys," it means He withdraws protection, permitting sin's results — not coercive violence. All interpretation must be cross-centered and Christocentric.

### 7. Movie Source: The Way of the Cross: Revelation and Discipleship
**ID:** `1d485e3c-24f2-461c-a035-6998c433774d` | Type: pasted_text
Argument outlines for Chs. 21–25 (Block 5). The cross is both God's supreme revelation and the pattern of life for followers. Ch. 25: the cross secures the universe forever — all intelligent beings freely trust God's character; eternal life is secure not by force but by freely given trust. This source was used for a previous video.

### 8. The Character of God and the Great Controversy
**ID:** `b8cf05e5-6e10-40ff-a9bd-2f62048c0cc9` | Type: pasted_text
Cosmic scope: the cross unveiled God's character to the universe. Even heavenly beings were unsettled until the cross clarified God's justice and love. God wins not by force but by truth and love; the cross breaks Satan's charges. The final message (Rev 18) finishes the work by reproducing that character in a people so the earth is lightened with His "glory" (character).

---

### 9. The Core of Divine Understanding
**ID:** `dcec39ff-ab26-48e4-ba01-f1b9e0c107a3` | Type: pasted_text
Covers Ch. 1 of the book. A person's concept of God is the most important element in religion — eternal life depends on knowing Him as He truly is (John 17:3). Israel's tragic reversal (crowned then crucified Jesus) stemmed from a false concept of God's character. False ideas distort worship, faith, and openness to truth. Knowledge of God must be relational, not speculative.

---

### 10. The Cross and the Final Showdown
**ID:** `2f8c37dd-d200-4aac-a46f-18d01e29a9e0` | Type: pasted_text
Argument outlines for Chs. 31–39. Ch. 31: the cross proves God's government is based on freedom, not force. Ch. 32: the cross is the fullest revelation of divine love — unconditional, extending even to enemies. Ch. 33: the cross is God's clearest demonstration of truth, refuting Satan's lies. Ch. 34: true divine power is self-sacrificial love, not domination.

---

### 11. The Cross: Final Issues and Showdown Arguments
**ID:** `78e1d834-378e-4726-8455-349e056805a2` | Type: pasted_text
Argument outlines for Chs. 26–30. Ch. 26: judgment revealed through the cross — God judges by truth and love, not arbitrary force. Ch. 30: the cross is decisive for the entire universe's confidence in God. Angels and unfallen beings watched Calvary; it answered their questions. Calvary revealed Satan's and God's true characters simultaneously, securing eternal loyalty and ensuring rebellion will never rise again.

---

### 12. The Cruciform Principle: Revelation, Life, and Loyalty
**ID:** `7b7f7a78-a890-49fe-9122-4d00466e1b28` | Type: pasted_text
The cross as an "infinite revelation of God's self-giving love." Two antagonistic crosses sharply contrasted: God's cross of love vs. Satan's counterfeit cross of coercion. The cross is primarily a revelation of character, not a legal transaction. Lucifer rejected the cross because it contradicted his principle of self-exaltation. Apostasy = following the way of the counterfeit cross (force, domination).
