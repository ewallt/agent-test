## Step 2 — Write the source document

### Document structure

**Title and overview**
- A short, specific title
- 2–4 sentences explaining what the video is about — the topic, the angle, and why it matters

**Scenes**

Each scene follows this pattern:

```
Scene N — [Short Descriptive Title]
[1–3 short paragraphs. One idea. Concrete and visual.]
```

Scenes should build like a story unfolding — each one moves the idea forward, not sideways.

**How many scenes:** Aim for 5–8. More shorter scenes is better than fewer longer ones. When in doubt, split.

**When to split a scene:**
- The scene is covering more than one distinct idea or moment
- The scene has shifted setting, time, or subject mid-paragraph
- The emotional or narrative climax of the piece deserves its own scene — always give it one

**Key takeaway**
- 2 sentences
- Summarizes the central idea — what the viewer should walk away with

## Example Structure (Adams and Jefferson)

```
# Adams and Jefferson: The Last Day

On July 4th, 1826... [2-sentence overview]

Scene 1 — Two Old Men, Far Apart
Adams was 90, in Quincy. Jefferson was 83, at Monticello...

Scene 2 — What They Had Been to Each Other
They had worked side by side in 1776...

Scene 3 — The Reconciliation
The letters began in 1812...

Scene 4 — The Fiftieth Year
July 4th, 1826 was a national celebration unlike any before it...

Scene 5 — The Same Afternoon
Jefferson died at 12:50. Adams died that evening...

Scene 6 — What the Coincidence Meant
When Americans learned both men had died on the same day...

Scene 7 — What Was Left Behind
A week before he died, Jefferson wrote what turned out to be his last letter...

Key Takeaway
Adams and Jefferson died on the same day — July 4th, 1826...
```

This example was validated in practice and produced a strong NLM cinematic video result.
