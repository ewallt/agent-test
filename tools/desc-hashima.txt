In April 1974, the residents of Hashima Island boarded a ferry and left. The island — a self-contained city of apartment towers, hospitals, a cinema, and schools built on open sea nine miles off Nagasaki — was empty within weeks. They did not strip the buildings. A calendar still shows April 1974. A child's shoe still sits on a corridor floor.

At its peak in 1959, Hashima held 5,259 people on a platform the size of a city block — the most densely populated place on earth. The coal ran out, and so did the city. The buildings are still standing. The seawall holds. This is not a ruin. It is a pause, and nobody told the buildings they were finished.
