Two islands sit three miles apart in the Bering Strait — close enough to see on most days, close enough on a calm winter to walk between them on the ice. Little Diomede is Alaska. Big Diomede is Russia. The International Date Line runs through the water between them. Standing on the American shore, you can see Tuesday and Wednesday at the same time.

For centuries, the Inupiaq and Yupik people crossed freely — trading, intermarrying, visiting family. In 1948 the Soviet Union expelled the entire civilian population of Big Diomede and closed the border. Families on opposite sides were permanently separated overnight. Today the crossing takes under an hour in a small boat and is effectively forbidden — no legal route exists. The ice still forms. The islands are still visible. The people who once crossed freely cannot cross at all.
