Tuvalu is a Pacific island nation of 11,000 people. Its average elevation is two meters above sea level. The ocean is rising at nearly four millimeters per year here — faster than the global average. During king tides, seawater seeps up through the ground and covers the roads. The water does not flood in. It comes up from below.

The Tuvaluan government has already negotiated relocation agreements with Australia and New Zealand. It is simultaneously arguing before international courts that it should retain sovereignty as a nation even after its land disappears — a legal question no court has ever ruled on. Tuvalu is not a metaphor for climate change. It is the place where climate change runs out of metaphors and becomes logistics.
