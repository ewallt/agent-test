The guns of the Western Front stopped on November 11, 1918. The shells did not. Between one and two billion artillery shells were fired into northeastern France during four years of war. A significant percentage never detonated. The French government quarantined hundreds of square miles into what is officially called the Zone Rouge. Nine villages were so completely destroyed they were never rebuilt.

More than a century later, French bomb-disposal teams still recover approximately 900 tons of unexploded munitions from the soil every year — what they call the Iron Harvest. The soil is saturated with arsenic at 300 times the safe threshold, along with lead, mercury, and chlorine compound residue from chemical weapons. The French government's official estimate for when the land will be fully safe: the year 2300. The war ended in 1918. It is not over.
