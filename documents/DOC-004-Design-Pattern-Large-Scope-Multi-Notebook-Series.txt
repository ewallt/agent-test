1. Pattern Definition
What Qualifies as Large Scope Multi-Notebook Series
A topic requires the Large Scope Multi-Notebook Series pattern when:

Breadth exceeds single notebook capacity — The topic encompasses too many distinct aspects, phases, or dimensions to cover adequately in 3-5 videos
Multiple natural subdivisions exist — The topic breaks cleanly into recognizable chunks (chronological periods, thematic categories, geographic regions, etc.)
Depth per subdivision is substantial — Each potential subdivision could sustain 3-5 videos on its own
Source material is abundant — Sufficient high-quality sources exist to support multiple distinct research queries
Recognition Criteria
Scope indicators:

Historical periods spanning years/decades/centuries
Scientific domains with multiple major sub-fields
Complex systems with numerous interacting components
Topics that naturally suggest "which aspect?" when mentioned
Scale assessment:

Duration (for historical topics): >5 years typically suggests series
Complexity: Multiple concurrent dimensions (military + political + economic + social)
Geographic spread: Multi-regional or global scope
Conventional treatment: How do textbooks/courses typically structure this topic?
When to Use This Pattern vs. Single Notebook
Use Single Notebook when:

Topic can be thoroughly covered in 3-5 videos
No natural subdivisions present themselves
Attempting to decompose would create artificial boundaries
Depth per subdivision would be too shallow
Use Multi-Notebook Series when:

Initial assessment suggests 8+ videos would be needed
Clear natural divisions exist
Each subdivision can sustain meaningful depth
User learning goals benefit from structured progression
2. Pattern Type: Major Historical Event/Period
Characteristics
Major Historical Events/Periods share these traits:

Temporal bounds — Defined beginning and end (even if fuzzy)
Chronological arc — Events unfold over time with causation
Multiple dimensions — Military, political, economic, social, cultural, technological aspects
Geographic scope — May span regions or be locally focused
Rich historiography — Multiple scholarly perspectives available
Lasting impact — Consequences worth exploring
Scale Assessment Framework
Estimate notebook count based on:

Duration:

1-10 years: 3-5 notebooks
10-50 years: 5-8 notebooks
50-200 years: 8-12 notebooks
200+ years: 12-20 notebooks
Geographic scope modifier:

Local/regional: baseline
Multi-regional: +1-2 notebooks
Continental: +2-3 notebooks
Global: +3-5 notebooks
Complexity modifier:

Single primary dimension: baseline
2-3 major dimensions: +1 notebook
4+ interwoven dimensions: +2-3 notebooks
Example: French Revolution (1789-1799)

Duration: 10 years → 3-5 notebooks baseline
Geographic: Primarily France, European spillover → +0-1
Complexity: Political + social + military + cultural → +1
Estimated range: 4-6 notebooks
Decomposition Strategies
Two primary approaches for Major Historical Events/Periods:

Strategy A: Chronological Decomposition
When to use:

Strong narrative arc exists
Distinct phases are recognizable
Causation flows clearly from period to period
Understanding sequence matters
Method:

Identify major phase boundaries (regime changes, key events, turning points)
Verify each phase can sustain 3-5 videos
Ensure phases are roughly balanced in importance/content
Consider whether to include origins/aftermath notebooks
Advantages:

Natural storytelling flow
Easy to follow
Shows causation clearly
Matches how most people learn history
Disadvantages:

May not deeply explore thematic threads
Can fragment related developments across notebooks
Geographic breadth in simultaneous events challenging
Strategy B: Thematic Decomposition
When to use:

Multiple parallel dimensions of equal importance
Chronological sequence less critical than understanding domains
Topic invites comparative analysis across dimensions
Themes persist across entire period
Method:

Identify major thematic dimensions (political, military, economic, social, cultural, intellectual, etc.)
Verify each theme can sustain 3-5 videos across the full period
Ensure themes are distinct enough to minimize overlap
Consider whether an overview/synthesis notebook is needed
Advantages:

Deep exploration of each dimension
Reveals patterns within domains
Allows specialist focus
Good for comparative analysis
Disadvantages:

Loses chronological narrative flow
May obscure causal connections between domains
Requires more synthesis work from viewer
Can feel repetitive if same events appear in multiple notebooks

Comparison: Chronological vs. Thematic
Chronological approach (5 notebooks):

Strengths: Natural narrative flow, shows causation, easier to follow
Weaknesses: Themes are fragmented across notebooks, requires repetition of context
Best for: Understanding sequence of events, seeing how revolution radicalized over time
Thematic approach (5 notebooks):

Strengths: Deep exploration of each dimension, comparative analysis within domains
Weaknesses: Loses chronological narrative, same events appear in multiple notebooks
Best for: Understanding structural transformations, analyzing specific aspects in depth
Recommendation for French Revolution: Chronological

The narrative arc is compelling and important
Phases are clearly defined
Understanding radicalization over time is key to understanding the Revolution
Thematic threads can be woven into chronological notebooks through good focus angle design
4. Application Template for Major Historical Events/Periods
Decision Tree
Step 1: Assess Scale

Calculate duration in years
Assess geographic scope
Count major dimensions
Estimate notebook range using formulas in Section 2
Step 2: Choose Decomposition Strategy

Ask: Does this topic have a strong chronological narrative with clear phases?

Yes → Default to chronological decomposition
No or Weak → Consider thematic decomposition
Ask: Are thematic dimensions equally important throughout the period?

Yes → Thematic decomposition may work better
No → Chronological likely better
Ask: Does understanding sequence and causation matter?

Critical → Chronological
Less important → Thematic could work
Step 3: Execute Decomposition

If Chronological:

Identify phase boundaries (regime changes, key events, wars, etc.)
Verify phases are roughly balanced
Ensure 3-5 videos per phase
Adjust boundaries if phases are too small/large
Consider adding origins and/or legacy notebooks
If Thematic:

List all major dimensions
Verify each can sustain 3-5 videos across full period
Check for overlap/redundancy
Consolidate if needed
Consider adding overview and/or synthesis notebooks
Step 4: Design Each Notebook

For each notebook in the series:

Topic naming: Include series name + specific focus
Video count: 3-5 based on content density
Depth: Usually "deep" for series notebooks
Guidance: Specific to that notebook's focus, reference the larger context
Step 5: Generate JSONs

Produce complete JSON for each notebook following the specification.

5. Variations and Edge Cases
When to Create Overview Notebook
Create an overview notebook when:

Series is large (6+ notebooks)
Topic is unfamiliar to general audiences
Chronology or structure needs introduction
Synthesis across notebooks would be valuable
Placement:

Usually first in chronological series
Sometimes last in thematic series (as synthesis)
Example: For a 10-notebook series on Roman Empire, an overview notebook covering "Rise, Peak, and Fall" would orient viewers before deep dives.

Handling Sub-Topics That Could Be Their Own Series
Example: The Holocaust within WW2, Napoleon within French Revolution

Options:

Integrate: Treat as one notebook within the larger series
Separate: Create standalone series, reference in main series
Hybrid: One notebook in main series, note that deeper treatment available separately
Decision factors:

How essential is sub-topic to understanding main topic?
How deep does treatment need to be?
User interest/time constraints
Geographic Decomposition
When chronological and thematic both feel wrong:

Some topics naturally divide by geography:

WW2 could be: European Theater, Pacific Theater, Home Fronts, Global Implications
Roman Empire could be: Republican Rome, Eastern Empire, Western Empire, Provinces
Use geographic decomposition when:

Events in different regions are largely independent
Regional differences are more important than temporal or thematic patterns
Geographic scope is the defining characteristic
Hybrid Approaches
Combining strategies within a series:

Example: WW2 series could use:

Chronological for military campaigns (Origins → Early War → Turning Point → Victory)
Thematic for specific dimensions (Holocaust, Home Fronts, Technology, Diplomacy)
When to use hybrid:

Very large series (8+ notebooks)
Some dimensions naturally chronological, others thematic
Allows both narrative flow and deep thematic exploration
Structure: Usually group chronological notebooks together, then thematic deep-dives

6. Scaling Examples
Applying This Pattern to Larger Topics
World War 2 (using same framework):

Scale assessment:

Duration: 6 years (1939-1945) → 3-5 baseline
Geographic: Global → +4
Complexity: Military + political + economic + social + technological + genocidal → +2
Estimated: 9-11 notebooks
Recommended approach: Chronological + Thematic Hybrid

Chronological core (6 notebooks):

Origins and Outbreak (1931-1939)
Axis Ascendancy (1939-1941)
Global War (1941-1942)
Turning Points (1942-1943)
Allied Victory in Europe (1943-1945)
Pacific War and Atomic Age (1941-1945)
Thematic deep-dives (4 notebooks): 7. The Holocaust and Genocide 8. Home Fronts and Total War 9. Technology and Innovation 10. Aftermath and New World Order

Roman Empire (even larger scope):

Scale assessment:

Duration: ~1000 years → 12-20 baseline
Geographic: Mediterranean-wide → +2
Complexity: Political + military + economic + social + cultural + religious → +2
Estimated: 16-24 notebooks (would likely recommend 12-15 for practical purposes)
Recommended approach: Chronological by Era

Each major period gets 2-3 notebooks:

Late Republic Crisis (133-27 BCE)
Augustus and the Principate
Julio-Claudian Dynasty
Year of Four Emperors and Flavians
The Five Good Emperors
Severan Dynasty and Crisis
Crisis of Third Century
Diocletian and Tetrarchy
Constantine and Christianization
Divided Empire
Western Collapse
Eastern Survival (Byzantine transition)
Applying to Non-Historical Large Scope Topics
College-Level Physics (topical domain, not historical):

Scale assessment:

Not temporal, but conceptual breadth
Major sub-fields: Mechanics, Thermodynamics, E&M, Waves/Optics, Modern Physics
Estimated: 5-6 notebooks
Recommended approach: Thematic by Sub-Field

Each sub-field is naturally thematic:

Classical Mechanics (kinematics, dynamics, energy, momentum)
Thermodynamics and Statistical Mechanics
Electromagnetism (electrostatics, circuits, magnetism, induction)
Waves, Optics, and Sound
Modern Physics I (relativity, quantum basics)
Modern Physics II (atomic, nuclear, particle physics)
Pattern adapts: Same decomposition logic, but dimensions are conceptual rather than temporal or thematic in historical sense.

7. Quality Checks Before Finalizing Series
Before generating final JSONs, verify:

Completeness:

[ ] Does the series cover all major aspects of the topic?
[ ] Are there obvious gaps?
Balance:

[ ] Are notebooks roughly similar in scope/depth?
[ ] Is one notebook trying to cover too much?
Coherence:

[ ] Do the notebooks form a logical set?
[ ] Is the organizing principle clear?
[ ] Would a viewer understand how notebooks relate?
Practical:

[ ] Is the total count reasonable (not overwhelming)?
[ ] Will NotebookLM find good sources for each notebook?
[ ] Does each notebook have enough distinct content for 3-5 videos?
Guidance quality:

[ ] Is guidance specific to each notebook's focus?
[ ] Does guidance help NotebookLM understand what to prioritize?
[ ] Have I avoided vague or generic guidance?
