  The phenomenon has two explanations: (1) psychological — confirmation bias and selective
  memory mean we notice when the other lane moves and forget when ours does; (2) statistical
  — slower lanes genuinely have more cars, so we are mathematically more likely to be in one.

  Video 1: The psychology angle — confirmation bias, selective attention, negativity bias.
  Video 2: The statistics angle — why slower lanes have more cars, the inspection paradox,
  the math working against us.
  Video 3: Broader examples of the same phenomenon — checkout lines, elevators, waiting rooms,
  download bars. Short intro stating the two underlying concepts (psychology + statistics),
  then a survey of other everyday cases. Keep intro tight.

  Prioritise sources that engage with the statistical/mathematical explanation — this is
  the more surprising and less well-known angle. Avoid purely anecdotal sources.
