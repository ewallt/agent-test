Ellen G. White on Preaching

What the Word Says About the Word

"Jesus is the living center of everything. Put Christ into every sermon. Let the preciousness, mercy, and glory of Jesus Christ be dwelt upon until Christ is formed within, the hope of glory."

— Ellen G. White

White taught that every sermon, whatever its topic, must have Christ as its living center. Doctrinal arguments without Christ fail to soften the soul. She warned against "dry and Christless" discourses, insisting that even when presenting the law, Christ must remain the great center of attraction. The burden of every message is "the science of salvation" — Christ crucified, risen, ascended, and coming again.

"It is through the Word — not feeling, not excitement — that we want to influence the people to obey the truth. On the platform of God's Word we can stand with safety."

— Ellen G. White

The Bible is the preacher's sole authority. White cautioned against stripping verses from context, interpreting symbols by personal imagination, or substituting human tradition and opinion for Scripture. She urged ministers to let the Word speak to the people rather than relying on ecstatic experience or emotional fervor. The living Word carries its own power — the preacher's task is to proclaim it faithfully.

"No man can rightly present the law of God without the gospel, or the gospel without the law."

— Ellen G. White

White saw the law and the gospel as inseparable partners in faithful preaching. The law reveals sin and drives the sinner to the Savior; the gospel provides the righteousness of Christ. To preach one without the other is to preach incompletely. She urged ministers to help sinners recognize their transgression of the law while exercising faith in Jesus — the old gospel doctrine of sorrow for sin, repentance, and confession.

"The preaching of the word should appeal to the intellect and should impart knowledge, but it should do more than this. The minister's utterances, to be effectual, must reach the hearts of his hearers."

— Ellen G. White

Information alone does not transform. White taught that while preaching must engage the mind, its ultimate aim is the heart. She warned against relying on amusing stories, sensationalism, or oratorical display — eloquence pursued for its own sake causes the truth to slip away. What the pulpit calls for is sincerity and deep earnestness, the minister standing as a voice from God, calm and dignified, speaking as one who believes every word.

"Ministers should present the sure word of prophecy, as the foundation of the faith of Seventh-day Adventists. The prophecies of Daniel and the Revelation should be carefully studied… We are today living in the time when the predictions of this chapter are fulfilling."

— Ellen G. White

White called ministers to be heralds of the Second Coming. The prophetic books — Daniel, Revelation, Matthew 24 — are not optional additions to the preaching calendar but essential content for the last days. She urged ministers to explain the prophecies to those they instruct, helping believers understand the time they live in. The return of Christ is the horizon that gives every sermon its urgency.
