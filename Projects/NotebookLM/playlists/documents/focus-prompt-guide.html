Focus Prompt Guide

How to write sharp, effective focus prompts for videos, slide decks, and infographics.

💡 The Core Principle

The focus string becomes the organizing spine of the artifact. Everything NotebookLM includes or cuts is shaped by it. A weak focus → generic output. A sharp focus → something worth watching or reading.

[Specific subject] + [Surprising angle or key tension] + [Why it matters]

You don't need all three every time, but the more of the formula you hit, the better the output.

✓ Good focus

Names something specific. Has an angle. Implies a question or tension to resolve. Sounds like a title you'd click.

✗ Bad focus

Starts with "Overview of…" or "Introduction to…". Could describe any article on the topic. No angle, no tension, no reason to care.

Key rules One sentence only — no compound ideas joined by "and also." Lead with the counterintuitive or surprising angle. Avoid generic framing. Source-isolate when you can: tying a focus to a specific source with --source-ids produces tighter, more accurate output.

Real examples

  • WW2 Pacific Japan's strategic miscalculation at Pearl Harbor — the missing carriers, the oil tanks left untouched, and why a tactical triumph became a strategic catastrophe
  • Eastern Front Hitler's hubris and the encirclement of the 6th Army at Stalingrad — why this battle is considered the single turning point of the Eastern Front
  • Scientific Revolutions: Chemistry How Mendeleev's periodic table revealed a hidden order in nature and predicted elements that didn't yet exist
  • The Malthusian Trap Why Malthus's logic held for all of human history, how the Industrial Revolution broke it, and why the escape's timing and location remain debated
  • Scientific Revolutions: Chemistry How Lavoisier's discovery of oxygen killed the phlogiston theory and launched modern chemistry
Pattern to notice Each example names the mechanism of change ("strategic miscalculation," "hubris and encirclement," "hidden order"), not just the subject. That mechanism is the angle — it tells NotebookLM what story to tell.

📊 Slide Decks (NLM-native)

Format options

presenter_slides
One point per slide, clean layout. Best for live presentations. Pairs well with a single strong theme.
detailed_deck
More content per slide. Better as a reference or leave-behind.

Length options

default
Standard length. Good starting point.
short
Fewer slides. Good when you want tight, punchy coverage.

The focus works exactly the same as for video — one tight sentence with a specific angle. The difference is that slides are sequential, so the focus sets the through-line that carries across every slide rather than just framing a narrative arc.

Tip presenter_slides + a focus that maps to a clear sequence of points (causes → event → consequences) produces the cleanest decks.

Writing the focus for an infographic

Infographics are visual summaries, not narratives. The focus should name the central organizing idea — what the whole thing is a picture of — rather than a story arc or tension to resolve.

Infographic focus ≠ video focus A video focus names a tension or surprising angle. An infographic focus names a structure: "The five stages of X," "What EGW says preachers should prioritize," "How Y changed between periods A and B." Think diagram caption, not documentary logline.
Solid default portrait + standard is a reliable starting point for most topics. Use detailed when the topic has many distinct components you want represented. Use landscape when the structure is inherently comparative.
