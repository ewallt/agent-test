NOTEBOOKLM FOCUS PROMPT GUIDE
==============================

THE CORE PRINCIPLE
------------------
The focus string becomes the organizing spine of the artifact. Everything
NotebookLM includes or cuts is shaped by it.

Formula:
  [Specific subject] + [Surprising angle or key tension] + [Why it matters]

GOOD focus: Names something specific. Has an angle. Implies a question or
tension to resolve. Sounds like a title you'd click.

BAD focus: Starts with "Overview of..." or "Introduction to...". Could
describe any article on the topic. No angle, no tension.

Key rules:
- One sentence only — no compound ideas joined by "and also"
- Lead with the counterintuitive or surprising angle
- Avoid generic framing
- Tie focus to a specific source with --source-ids for tighter output


VIDEOS
------
Style options (retro_print is the proven default):
  retro_print   Vintage print aesthetic. Works across topics.
  whiteboard    Clean hand-drawn. Good for explanations.
  heritage      Classic, editorial. Good for historical topics.
  watercolor    Soft, painterly. Good for natural/cultural topics.
  paper_craft   Cut-paper look. Distinctive.
  kawaii/anime  Playful/illustrated. Topic-dependent.
  classic       Standard clean style.
  auto_select   NotebookLM picks — unpredictable, avoid.

Format options:
  explainer     Default. Full narrative arc. Best for most topics.
  brief         Shorter, punchier. Good for a single tight insight.

Real examples:
  "Japan's strategic miscalculation at Pearl Harbor — the missing carriers,
  the oil tanks left untouched, and why a tactical triumph became a
  strategic catastrophe"

  "Hitler's hubris and the encirclement of the 6th Army at Stalingrad —
  why this battle is considered the single turning point of the Eastern Front"

  "How Mendeleev's periodic table revealed a hidden order in nature and
  predicted elements that didn't yet exist"

  "Why Malthus's logic held for all of human history, how the Industrial
  Revolution broke it, and why the escape's timing and location remain debated"

  "How Lavoisier's discovery of oxygen killed the phlogiston theory and
  launched modern chemistry"

Pattern to notice: each example names the mechanism of change, not just
the subject. That mechanism is the angle.


SLIDE DECKS (NLM-native)
------------------------
Format options:
  presenter_slides  One point per slide, clean. Best for live presentations.
  detailed_deck     More content per slide. Better as a reference/leave-behind.

Length options:
  default   Standard length.
  short     Fewer slides. Good for tight, punchy coverage.

Focus works exactly the same as video — one tight sentence with a specific
angle. presenter_slides + a focus that maps to a clear sequence (causes →
event → consequences) produces the cleanest decks.


INFOGRAPHICS
------------
Orientation options:
  portrait    Tall. Best for timelines, lists, sequential flows. Good default.
  landscape   Wide. Good for comparisons, side-by-side structures.
  square      Social-friendly. Good for single central ideas.

Detail options:
  concise   Fewer elements, more white space. Good for at-a-glance.
  standard  Balanced. Good default.
  detailed  Dense, rich. Good for complex topics with many components.

IMPORTANT: Infographic focus ≠ video focus.
  Video focus   → names a tension or surprising angle (documentary logline)
  Infographic   → names a structure or central organizing idea (diagram caption)

Examples of infographic-style focus:
  "The five stages of X"
  "What EGW says preachers should prioritize"
  "How Y changed between periods A and B"

Solid default: portrait + standard for most topics.
Use detailed when the topic has many distinct components.
Use landscape when the structure is inherently comparative.


QUICK REFERENCE
---------------
  Video        style: retro_print  |  format: explainer
  Slide deck   format: presenter_slides  |  length: default
  Infographic  orientation: portrait  |  detail: standard
