# NotebookLM Cinematic Video — Scene Feedback Evaluation

## Context

When a scene-format source is written for NLM cinematic video, AI feedback on the draft is a useful step before upload. This document covers how to evaluate that feedback: what to accept, what to reject, and the patterns that appear consistently across theological and educational content.

---

## The Core Question

For every piece of feedback, ask one question: **does this suggestion surface something already in the source text, or does it add something that isn't there?**

- **Surface** → accept
- **Add** → reject

This single rule handles most cases. The elaborations below describe what "surfacing" and "adding" look like in practice.

---

## What to Accept

### 1. Physical anchors for abstract statements

If a scene describes a theological concept without placing someone in a specific location doing something, feedback that grounds it in a physical body is almost always right. Accept suggestions that:

- Identify the person who would be present when this idea is communicated
- Assign them a specific location (a room, a desk, a street)
- Give them a visible action (writing, looking up, lifting their head)

The feedback is doing the abstract→physical translation work described in the scene writing guidelines. It should be accepted when the physical situation it proposes is consistent with the source material.

### 2. Positive rewrites of negations

Negations describe the absence of something. Veo 3 cannot render an absence. Feedback that rewrites a negation as a positive visible state is technically necessary and should be accepted.

Common patterns:

| Original | Rewrite direction |
|---|---|
| "He is no longer under law" | A door swinging open, not a locked door |
| "Not empty" | Open hands, present and visible |
| "Without strength" | A figure who cannot lift herself — not a figure described as lacking something |

Accept rewrites that make the state filmable, even if the original phrasing is accurate to the source.

### 3. Through-line subject consistency

If a source has a recurring physical subject (a man at a desk, a woman in a synagogue) and feedback suggests bringing that subject back into scenes where it has drifted away, accept it. The through-line subject gives Veo 3 something consistent to return to between ideas and prevents each scene from feeling like it starts over.

Specific form this takes: feedback notices that Scene 3 or 4 has moved away from the established subject and suggests re-anchoring. Accept this.

---

## What to Reject

### Invented imagery

AI feedback frequently adds period-appropriate props, gestures, or environments that are not in the source text. These should be rejected.

The source material — especially theological writing like Waggoner — typically already contains the right images. The work is to surface them, not to supplement them.

**Examples of rejected feedback from this project:**

- Scene depicting three things stripped from a man → AI suggested a king, a warrior, and a merchant. Rejected. Waggoner's own text already had the images: "all flesh is grass" (field bending in wind), "riches make themselves wings; they fly away as an eagle" (eagle lifting from branch), a balance tipping to nothing. Use those.
- Scene depicting an exchange → AI suggested "reaches across the dusty floor." Rejected. Not in the text; also wrong posture for the Gospel account. The actual gesture (laying hands on) was more specific and more filmable.

**The pattern:** AI feedback adds context to fill gaps it perceives. When the source material already has specific images, the gap is not real — the feedback is inventing where it should be reading.

---

## The Evaluation Sequence

1. Read each piece of feedback in isolation
2. For each suggestion, locate the corresponding passage in the source text
3. If the suggestion surfaces a physical detail already in the text → accept
4. If the suggestion adds something not in the text → reject, and check whether the source text has its own image that can be used instead
5. Apply accepted edits before uploading — do not upload the pre-feedback draft

---

## Relationship to Scene Writing Guidelines

This document covers the feedback evaluation step. The scene writing guidelines (`nlm-cinematic-scene-guidelines.md`) cover how to write scenes correctly in the first place.

The two documents are complementary. Writing guidelines reduce the number of feedback edits needed. Feedback evaluation ensures the remaining edits improve the source rather than dilute it.
