# The Promise Before the Law: Abraham and the Everlasting Covenant

When Paul wrote to the Galatians that "the Scripture preached the Gospel to Abraham," he was making a claim that the New Covenant was not new — it was the original promise, given 430 years before the law at Sinai. This video traces that promise from its origin in a field at night, through the shadow of Moriah, to the cross where what was sworn to one man was finally opened to all nations.

---

Scene 1 — The Night God Made a Promise

A lean, weathered man stands outside his tent in the Canaanite night. The desert air is still. His head tilts back. The stars above him are without number — they fill the sky from edge to edge, and as his eyes move across them his figure grows smaller against the dark, a single man swallowed by the immensity above.

God speaks to him: count them, if you can. So shall your descendants be. And the text records the simplest sentence: Abraham believed God. That belief — nothing more — was counted to him as righteousness. He made no promise. He performed no act. The wind moves through the dry grass at his feet. He simply believed.

---

Scene 2 — The Covenant No Man Could Break

The animals lie in two rows in the dark field — a heifer, a goat, a ram, each divided in two, the pieces facing each other across a narrow corridor of bare earth. This was the ancient ceremony of covenant: both parties walk through together, swearing the oath with their bodies. Abraham arranged the pieces and waited. As the sun set, a deep sleep came over him and a thick darkness settled around him. He lay still on the ground.

Then a smoking furnace and a burning torch appeared in the night and moved slowly through the corridor between the pieces. One flame, moving through darkness. God alone. Abraham lay asleep. God swore the covenant to himself — no second party, no counter-obligation, no human pledge. What passes through that dark field moves unhurried, as if the outcome were never in doubt. What was sworn here cannot be broken by human failure, because no human made it.

---

Scene 3 — The Wood on His Son's Back

The path up the mountain is steep and strewn with loose rock. Abraham walks a few paces behind his son — Isaac — who carries a bundle of cut wood on his back, the weight shifting with each step as he climbs. Their breathing is audible in the stillness. Neither speaks. The rocky slope passes slowly beneath their feet as they go up together.

Isaac asks: the fire and the wood are here — but where is the lamb? Abraham answers: God will provide himself a lamb, my son. They continue upward. At the summit Isaac lays down the wood and lies upon it willingly. Abraham raises the knife. An angel calls from heaven: do not touch the boy. Abraham looks up and sees a ram caught by its horns in a thicket. God provides the substitute. Before they descend, God swears again — by an oath, by himself, the highest authority that exists — that in Abraham's seed all nations of the earth will be blessed.

---

Scene 4 — Two Months Later, a Different Mountain

Mount Sinai blazes with fire. Smoke pours from the peak and rolls down the slope. Thunder moves through the cloud. The people of Israel are gathered on the plain below — hundreds of thousands of them, stretching back from the mountain's base across the flat ground — and they have been warned not to approach the boundary. The mountain fills the sky above them, and below it the crowd is a single mass, every face turned upward.

Moses descends with the terms of the covenant. The whole congregation answers together, as one voice: "All that the Lord hath spoken, we will do." Not one dissents. Their confidence is complete.

It is the only promise Israel will make that it will break, systematically, for the next thousand years. Abraham said nothing. Israel said everything. Israel's covenant was with their own ability — which was no foundation at all.

---

Scene 5 — The Hill Outside Jerusalem

The sky above Golgotha has gone dark. It is the middle of the day and there is no sun. A man hangs on a wooden cross against the blackened sky, and the few people who have not fled stand some distance off, their faces turned toward him. The wind moves through the hill. Nothing else moves.

This is where the curse of the law is lifted. Paul writes: Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us — that the blessing of Abraham might come on the Gentiles. What was sworn to one man in a dark field, confirmed in blood on Moriah, and carried unbroken through four thousand years of human failure — arrives here. The blessing of Abraham does not come through the law. It comes through this.

---

Scene 6 — Written in a Different Place

A stone room, evening. A single lamp burns on a low table. A man sits alone, head bowed, an open scroll before him — but his hands are still in his lap and his eyes are not moving across the text. The lamplight catches his face. Dust drifts slowly through the air above the flame. The room is quiet.

Paul writes that the new covenant is not written on stone tablets but on hearts of flesh. The promise given to Abraham was never mediated by the law — it was given 430 years before the law existed, and when the law came, it could not alter what had already been sworn. Those who are in Christ are Abraham's seed, heirs according to the promise. The covenant God swore to himself in a dark field has not changed. It has finally arrived.

---

Key Takeaway
The New Covenant is not a correction of the old — it is the original promise, the one God swore to Abraham before circumcision, before the law, before Sinai. The cross is not where God changed his plan; it is where he kept it.
