# Mary Cassatt: The American Bridge

Cassatt essentially curated what became the great American museum collections. Her wealthy friends the Havemeyers bought Impressionist work on her advice. What is now at the Metropolitan Museum, the Art Institute of Chicago, and the National Gallery arrived in America because of her taste and persuasion.

---

Scene 1 — The Dealer's Window, 1875

A young American woman — early 20s — stands before a painting in a Paris dealer's window. The dealer watches her from inside. She is still, studying the canvas: a Degas pastel, dancers caught mid-motion. She goes inside. The camera moves with her through the door into the gallery interior, then holds as she stands before the pastel and an older woman — Cassatt — says something quietly beside her. The dealer waits.

SETTING: An art dealer's gallery near the Rue de Rivoli, Paris, 1875
SUBJECT: Louisine Elder (later Havemeyer) and Mary Cassatt before a Degas pastel
IN-SCENE MOTION: She enters, crosses to the painting; Cassatt leans close and speaks; the dealer watches
LIGHTING: Gallery daylight from large windows; the pastel is lit from slightly above
NARRATION BEAT: In 1875, Cassatt guided her friend Louisine Elder to buy a Degas pastel for 500 francs. It was among the first Impressionist works purchased by an American. Elder would later marry H.O. Havemeyer.

---

Scene 2 — The Letters

Cassatt sits at a writing desk in her Paris apartment, writing by lamplight. The camera slowly orbits her, tracking from left to right. Stacks of letters and reply envelopes surround her. She writes quickly, moves to the next sheet. Outside the window behind her, Paris at night — a faint glow above the roofline.

SETTING: Cassatt's Paris apartment, late 1880s–1890s
SUBJECT: Cassatt writing advisory letters to American collectors
IN-SCENE MOTION: Her pen moves across sheets; she turns pages; a lamp flame shifts slightly
LIGHTING: Warm lamp glow in a dark room; the window shows faint city light
NARRATION BEAT: For decades, Cassatt advised American friends and collectors — by letter, in person, and through dealers — on what to buy. Monet, Pissarro, Renoir, Cézanne. She had access; they had money.

---

Scene 3 — The Crates Arrive

A New York shipping dock, winter. A large wooden crate is lowered by rope from a warehouse platform. Workers with crowbars wait on the dock below. Steam rises from the river behind them. A man pulls the straw away from the canvas inside and holds a lamp close: a Monet, one of the Haystacks series, painted across the seasons. He says nothing. The camera pushes in slowly on the revealed surface.

SETTING: New York Harbor, 1890s
SUBJECT: Impressionist paintings arriving from France
IN-SCENE MOTION: Crate lowered by crane; straw pulled away; lamp brought close; the men are still
LIGHTING: Winter grey outside; warm lamplight illuminating the canvas surface
NARRATION BEAT: Cassatt's recommendations crossed the Atlantic as physical objects — crates at docks, packed in straw. What the Metropolitan Museum now has from the Havemeyer collection came, in large part, through her.

---

Scene 4 — The Collection

A grand New York townhouse parlor, 1900. Paintings cover the walls from floor to ceiling. A Degas bronze stands on a low table. Louisine Havemeyer moves from canvas to canvas with a visitor, explaining each one in low tones. The camera tracks with her slowly. She stops before a large Cassatt — a mother and two children in afternoon light — and is quiet for a moment before she speaks.

SETTING: The Havemeyer residence, New York, early 1900s
SUBJECT: Louisine Havemeyer with her Impressionist collection
IN-SCENE MOTION: She walks, gestures toward canvases, stops before the Cassatt
LIGHTING: Interior parlor light — wall sconces and afternoon light through heavy drapes
NARRATION BEAT: The Havemeyer collection eventually numbered nearly 2,000 objects. When Louisine donated it to the Metropolitan Museum in 1929, it permanently changed what American audiences could see.

---

Scene 5 — The Bridge

Cassatt stands at the rail of an ocean liner, watching France recede into morning fog. She is returning to Paris after a visit to America. The camera tracks level with her along the rail as the ship moves. Her coat moves in the wind. Behind her, America. Ahead, the mist. She does not look at the camera.

SETTING: An ocean liner, mid-Atlantic, early 1900s
SUBJECT: Cassatt crossing between her two worlds
IN-SCENE MOTION: Ship moves through grey water; fog drifts past; her coat and hair move in the wind
LIGHTING: Flat grey ocean morning; no distinct shadow direction; diffuse and even
NARRATION BEAT: Cassatt lived most of her adult life in France but remained American by passport and connection. She spent her career moving between worlds — building the bridge that neither world fully recognized she was building.
