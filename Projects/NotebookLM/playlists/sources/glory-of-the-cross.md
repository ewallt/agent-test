# The Glory of the Cross: Galatians 6:12–18

E.J. Waggoner's study of Galatians 6:12–18 follows a man stripped of everything until only one thing remains — and then shows that one thing holding up the universe.

---

Scene 1 — The Hand That Wrote It

A man writes by lamplight, straining forward. His eyes are weak. He has always used an amanuensis — someone to write while he dictates. Not this time. He seized the pen and wrote every word himself. That decision is itself the argument: what he is about to say is too urgent to hand off.

On one side of the room, men are making a fair show. Marks on the flesh. Credentials. Outward signs of belonging. On the other side: a cross — which the world regards as the mark of a criminal.

---

Scene 2 — Three Things That Vanish

Take a man and remove what he might boast about.

A field of grass bends in the wind, and then it is gone — that is all flesh. An eagle lifts from a branch, circles once, and is a speck against the sky — that is riches. A balance is set on the table, and when wisdom is placed on one side, the scale tips to nothing. Strip all three away and what is left? Nothing that was his to begin with. Everything a man is or has came from somewhere else.

---

Scene 3 — The One Exception

The man at the desk sets down his pen. He has been writing about two texts from Scripture. They look different at first — Jeremiah says: glory in knowing Me; Paul says: glory only in the cross. But as he holds them side by side in the lamplight, they say the same thing.

To know the cross is to know God. There is no knowledge of God apart from it. The cross is not an event on the way to something else — it is the place where God is revealed.

---

Scene 4 — The Cross Cuts

The cross in which Paul glories is not a symbol. It is the cross on which Christ was executed. To Paul, it meant the same: the world is crucified to him, and he to the world.

A man walks through a city — its streets, its noise, its faces. He is in it but no longer of it. The world's friendship is enmity with God; the world's hatred is friendship with God. The man the world turns from carries the mark the world despises. That is the mark of the cross. That is the mark of belonging to Someone.

---

Scene 5 — Down, Then Up

He humbled Himself. To death. To the death of the cross. A seed pressed down into dark earth — cold, still, buried. Then: light breaking upward through soil, a shoot rising toward the sky, far above where it began.

Wherefore God highly exalted Him — far above all heavens. The same one who descended is the one who ascended. The cross was not the bottom of a pit with no exit. It was the turning point. The very thing that brought Him down is the only force that carries from earth to heaven.

---

Scene 6 — Every Ray of Light

The power of God is visible in created things. The cross is the power of God. Therefore the cross is visible in created things.

Every blade of grass growing in cursed soil. Every plant pushing through the dark. Every shaft of light reaching the ground. None of it could exist if Christ — who can die and still live — were not present everywhere, sustaining it. The heavens declare the glory of God. That glory, Waggoner says, is the glory of the cross. The stars are lit by it. Not a single ray of light reaches the earth except through the One who was lifted up.

---

Scene 7 — Branded

Paul closes the letter with his body.

He bears branded on his flesh the marks of Jesus. The word he uses is the word for a slave's brand — the mark an owner puts on property. He is not his own. He was bought. Let no man interfere with him. Let no man trouble him. He serves one Master, and his body carries the proof.

The letter opened with a man straining to write in lamplight. It closes with the marks that explain why he had to write it himself. He was not recording someone else's argument. He was testifying to what had been done to him.

---

Key Takeaway

A man stripped of wisdom, might, and riches arrives at nothing he can call his own — and then finds the cross. Waggoner's argument is that this is not a consolation prize. The cross is where God is known. It sustains every ray of light. It is the only force that descends into dark earth and rises to heaven. What the world marks as disgrace, the man who carries it calls glory — because the world's hatred and God's friendship are the same thing seen from opposite sides.
