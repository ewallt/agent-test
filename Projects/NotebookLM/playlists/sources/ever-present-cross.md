# The Ever-Present Cross: Galatians 2:17–21; 3:1

E.J. Waggoner's study of Galatians 2:17–21 turns on a single spatial claim: the cross of Christ is not behind you, and not ahead of you. It is here, where you are, where your sin is — and Christ is already bearing it.

---

Scene 1 — The Law's Honest Verdict

A man sits at a table with a document before him. The document lists what he owes — plainly, without exaggeration, without mercy. He reads it. He has no argument with it. The fault is not in the document; the fault is in him. He cannot pay.

This is where the law leaves everyone. It does not lie. It does not compromise. It points out what is owed and waits. Waggoner's point is that the law is not the enemy — it is the mirror. The problem is not the reflection; the problem is the man who looks into it and walks away unchanged, because he has no power to change.

---

Scene 2 — Dead to the Law, Raised as Another

The law's penalty falls on the man at the desk. It is satisfied — not because it softened, but because its demand was met in full. And something shifts. The man is still in the same room, at the same desk. But in the law's eyes, something has changed entirely. The record it held against him yields nothing. It reads the same document and finds no charge. He is not the man it condemned. He has, through the cross, become another person — the same body, the same room, but a new man in the most complete sense.

Paul says this is what happens in Christ. Through the cross he has died to the law. Through the resurrection he walks in newness of life. The law that once condemned him will, now that Christ is in him, bear witness to his righteousness — for Christ in the heart says, "I delight to do Thy will; Thy law is within My heart."

---

Scene 3 — The Cross Is Not Behind You

Here is the claim Waggoner makes that reorients everything: the cross is not a fixed point in the past. To look backward at Calvary and think it must somehow be appropriated from this distance — is to miss it. And to look forward, waiting for a saving act yet to come — is equally to miss it. The shadow of the cross stretches across all of time, from Paradise lost to Paradise restored. Its light falls directly on the desk where the man sits today.

Where, then, should he look? Not back, not forward — upward. The cross is present, reaching down to where he stands. Christ crucified is not a memory or a promise. He is a present reality, and the evidence is as close as the room he sits in.

---

Scene 4 — Sin Is Where I Am

The man at the desk cannot gather his debt and carry it somewhere else. Wherever he goes, it is with him. It is in him — in the heart, committed there, dwelling there, woven through the fabric of daily life. He cannot separate it from himself even by a hair's breadth. Every effort to make himself righteous from the outside is unavailing, because the problem is not the outside.

Whoever bears his sins must come where his sins are. He must enter the same room. He must come where sin lives.

---

Scene 5 — Christ Already There

There is a light in the room that the man at the desk has not yet turned to see. It has been there the whole time — present, steady, already bearing the weight he cannot carry. This is where Waggoner arrives at the center of the argument: Christ is already there. The risen and crucified Saviour is present in every person. He has come where sin is. He is bearing it now.

The difference between the sinner and the Christian is not that Christ is present in one and absent in the other. Christ is present in both. In the sinner He is there unrecognized — a light in the corner, unacknowledged. In the Christian He dwells there by faith: the man at the desk turns, sees the light, and receives it as his own.

---

Scene 6 — The Whole Gift for Each

The man steps outside, and the full light of the sun falls on him. It is the same light that falls on every other person — undivided, complete for each one, as though no one else were standing in it.

Waggoner pauses at Paul's singular: "who loved me, and gave Himself for me." Not for us — for me. The gift is not rationed among millions. Each soul receives the whole of Christ, as if there were no other person in the world. The sun shines for each one with its entire force. So Christ gave Himself for each individual soul — undivided, inexhaustible, entirely personal.

---

Scene 7 — Before Their Eyes

Paul asks the Galatians a pointed question: who bewitched you — you, before whose eyes Jesus Christ was openly set forth crucified? He is not describing vivid rhetoric. He is saying that when he preached among them, Christ was actually there, crucified, visible to those with eyes to see.

Waggoner adds: Paul avoided eloquence deliberately, because he did not want beautiful speech to substitute for presence. The cross is not made real by skilled words. It is real already. Each blade of grass, each leaf in the forest, each shaft of light through the canopy carries the testimony. And for anyone who has known what it is to be found and freed — the evidence is not outside at all. It is in them.

---

Key Takeaway

On the road to Galatia, men were looking for the cross in the wrong direction — backward to a finished event, or forward to a future completion. Waggoner's answer is that the cross was with them all along. In the room. At the desk. The light already burning in the corner. The question is not how to reach what is far away. It is whether the man at the desk will turn and see what has been there the whole time.
