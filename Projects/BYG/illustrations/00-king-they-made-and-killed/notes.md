# Illustration 00 — The King They Made and Killed

*Prologue illustration. Chapter 1: "An All-Important Theme."*

---

## The Illustration

**The story:**
Israel is under Roman occupation. They are waiting for the Messiah — but their concept of the Messiah is shaped by their concept of God: a conquering king who will defeat Rome by force.

When Jesus feeds 5,000 people with five loaves and two fish, the crowd sees the power they've been waiting for. They try to seize Him and make Him king — by force. Jesus withdraws.

Later, when the authorities want Him killed, the charge they use is: *claiming to be King.* Pilate asks, "Are you the King of the Jews?"

The ironies:
1. They tried to force Him into kingship — He refused.
2. They killed Him for claiming to be king — the very thing they had tried to make Him.
3. They wanted Him to use force against Rome — they became the force that killed Him.
4. They couldn't tolerate a God who wouldn't use force on their behalf, so they used force to destroy Him.

**Root cause:**
All of it flows from one thing: a false concept of God's character. They projected their own spirit of force onto God and designed a Messiah in that image. When the real Messiah appeared — revealing a God who governs by love, not compulsion — they couldn't receive Him.

This is Ch. 1's argument made flesh: *our concept of God governs everything. A wrong concept leads to fatal outcomes — even destroying the God you claim to worship.*

## Video Script

**Format:** Animated SVG doodles, dark background, per-bullet spring reveals. 4 bullet beats + title card. Estimated duration: ~60–75 seconds.

---

### Title Card
**Text:** "The King They Made and Killed"
**Subtext:** "What Israel's fatal mistake reveals about our concept of God"

---

### Bullet 1 — The Expectation
**Narration:** "Israel was under Rome. They were waiting for a Messiah — a conquering king who would defeat their enemies by force."

**Doodle concept:**
- Left side: Roman soldier silhouette (simple, imposing)
- Right side: Israeli figure looking upward expectantly, a crown shape hovering above (dotted, anticipatory)
- Visual language: oppression on one side, longing on the other

---

### Bullet 2 — The Power They Saw
**Narration:** "When Jesus fed five thousand people from almost nothing, the crowd recognized power. They tried to take Him by force and make Him king."

**Doodle concept:**
- Jesus figure at center, small loaves/fish nearby
- Crowd of hands reaching toward Him, pushing a crown toward His head
- Jesus figure stepping back / turning away — the crowd's arms extended but not reaching Him
- Motion: crowd surges forward, Jesus withdraws

---

### Bullet 3 — The Charge They Used
**Narration:** "Later, when they wanted Him dead, the charge they brought to Pilate was this: He claims to be King of the Jews."

**Doodle concept:**
- Split panel:
  - Left: crowd with crown (b[1] scene) — "MAKE HIM KING"
  - Right: accusers pointing at Jesus before Pilate — "HE CLAIMS TO BE KING"
- Arrow connecting the two panels — same word, opposite intent
- Visual irony made visible on screen

---

### Bullet 4 — The Irony
**Narration:** "They tried to force Him to be king. Then they killed Him for being king. They wanted a God who would use force — so they used force against God."

**Doodle concept:**
- God/Jesus figure in center
- Left: crowd offering crown with aggressive gesture (force toward)
- Right: crowd with cross / executing figure (force against)
- Same crowd, same posture, opposite action
- Optional: small label under each — "TRIED TO CROWN" / "CHOSE TO KILL"

---

### Bullet 5 — The Root Cause
**Narration:** "It all came from one thing: a false concept of God. They had designed a Messiah in their own image — a God of force. The real God didn't match the picture. So they rejected Him."

**Doodle concept:**
- Two side-by-side "portraits":
  - Left: a king on a throne holding a sword — "Their God"
  - Right: a servant figure washing feet or healing — "The Real God"
- X drawn through the left portrait with b[2] reveal
- Arrow pointing to right portrait brightens

---

### Closing Beat
**Text overlay:** *"Our concept of God governs everything."*
*(From Ch. 1)*
