Useful elements from the Deep Research report

Recommended source

Use the Our World in Data indicator “Number of people living in extreme poverty,” sourced from the World Bank Poverty and Inequality Platform. This is the best single coherent dataset for a bar chart race because it is already structured as a country-year series and measures counts of people rather than percentages.

Recommended unit

Millions of people

Recommended final CSV structure

model,lab,date,value

Formatting rules implied by the report

model should be the country name.

lab should be the same as the country name.

date should be converted from the milestone years into full unique dates in YYYY-MM-DD format.

value should be numeric only, expressed in millions of people.

Rows should remain in chronological order.

Every row must have a unique date, even when multiple countries share the same year.

Recommended dataset workflow

Use the OWID country-year series as the base dataset.

Filter to the 10 selected countries.

Filter to the six milestone years.

Convert raw people counts into millions of people.

Then convert year-only entries into unique full dates for the app.

Recommended time span logic

Use a modern policy-history arc rather than the full historical range.

The report treats 1990 to a recent endpoint as the best narrative window.

The selected milestone years are meant to keep the final table near 60 rows while preserving a clear long-run story.

Recommended story logic

The race should likely begin with very large poverty counts in major Asian countries.

A major part of the story is the decline in poverty counts in large Asian countries over time.

Another major part of the story is the increasing prominence of Sub-Saharan African countries in later years.

Possible story moments should be chosen after the numeric data are extracted, especially around:
dominance in the early years,
sharp decline by a major country,
rank shifts,
and growing African concentration in later years.

Important caveats from the report

These are estimated poverty counts, not direct census-like yearly counts.

The series depends on household survey data.

Some years may involve interpolation, extrapolation, or modeled alignment across surveys.

Income-based and consumption-based measures are not identical, though OWID simplifies to one country-year observation for charting.

PPP revisions matter, so values should not be mixed across incompatible poverty-line vintages.

The chart measures absolute numbers of people in extreme poverty, not poverty rates.

Most important limitation

The report does not contain the actual numeric country-year values needed to populate the value column. It provides the structure, source choice, countries, years, and caveats, but not the extracted numbers themselves.
