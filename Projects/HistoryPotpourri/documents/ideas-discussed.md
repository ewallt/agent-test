## Tier 1 — Strong, ready to write

- **Vasili Arkhipov / Soviet submarine (1962)** — Cuban Missile Crisis. U.S. depth charge on a Soviet sub in radio silence. Two of three officers wanted to launch a nuclear warhead; Arkhipov refused. Unanimous consent required. He alone prevented nuclear war.
- **Spanish Armada destroyed by storm (1588)** — Philip II's "invincible" fleet outmaneuvered by smaller English ships, then wrecked by storms rounding Scotland and Ireland on the way home.
- **Ogedei Khan's death saves Western Europe (1242)** — Mongols had swept through Poland and Hungary, entering Bohemia. Word arrived that the Great Khan had died; commanders withdrew for the kurultai. Western Europe spared.
- **Washington survives Braddock's Defeat (1755)** — Seven Years' War. Washington was bedridden with fever in a wagon. Two horses shot out from under him, four bullets through his coat. Walked off the battlefield untouched.
- **Fourth Crusade sacks Constantinople (1204)** — Crusaders bound for Jerusalem; diverted when a deposed Byzantine prince offered to fund them if they restored him to the throne. He was installed but couldn't pay. Crusaders sacked Constantinople instead, fatally crippling the Christian Byzantine Empire.
- **Flaming camels defeat war elephants (1398)** — Timur attacks Delhi. Sultan's army rides chain-mail-clad elephants; Timur's troops terrified. He loads hay on camels, sets them on fire, drives them into the enemy lines. Elephants scatter. Delhi falls.
- **Pax Mongolica accidentally spreads the Black Death (14th century)** — The Mongol conquests created the safest long-distance trade routes in history. That same safety let plague-bearing rat fleas hitchhike across Eurasia. The peace that killed Europe.
- **Humayun's staircase (1555)** — Mughal emperor spent 15 years in exile, won his empire back, died months later tripping on a steep staircase.

## Tier 2 — Decent, but thinner or harder to dramatize

- **Fall of Constantinople → Columbus → America (1492)** — Ottomans seize Constantinople (1453), control the Silk Roads, force Europeans to seek new sea routes. Spain funds Columbus. He finds the Americas. True causal chain, but sprawls across 40 years — needs tight framing to work as a single hinge.
- **Emperor Huizong paints cranes, gets captured (1112)** — Song dynasty artist-emperor painted cranes over his palace as a divine omen of long, glorious reign. Within years his capital was sacked by the Jin and he spent his last 20 years as a captive. Good irony; the gap between painting and capture makes the connection feel loose on screen.
- **Seabirds break the Dutch spice monopoly (17th century)** — Dutch ruthlessly controlled nutmeg and clove supply from the Banda Islands. Winds and seabirds carried seeds to other islands; rival merchants bought outside Dutch jurisdiction. Charming, but seed dispersal is hard to dramatize as a single moment.

## Tier 3 — Interesting but needs source material

- **Timur dies before invading China (1405)** — After Persia, India, and Anatolia, Timur marched east in December 1404. Fell ill and died February 1405. Invasion never began. Need source details on the Ming emissaries and the march.
- **Ain Jalut: Hulagu withdraws 90% of forces (1260)** — Hulagu destroyed Baghdad and Damascus, demanded Mamluk surrender. Recalled for a kurultai; left 20,000 under Kitbuqa. Mamluks crushed them at Ain Jalut — Mongol expansion halted permanently.
