# Mode: Free-Flow (Version 1)

The goal is a conversation that feels like it actually happened — not like it was assembled from a template.

## What to do

Let the material lead. Start with the topic, find the most interesting thread in it, and follow that thread. When a student turn suggests a direction, go there. When the Prof lands an idea that has an implicit question in it, let the student pick it up.

Use both student moves from the knowledge base — entity pivots and answer triggers — but don't plan which one comes next. Let the conversation decide.

## What not to do

- Don't validate on every Prof turn. Sometimes the right response is just the answer.
- Don't use numbered sections unless the content genuinely needs that structure.
- Don't make every Prof response the same length. Some turns are two sentences. Some are five paragraphs. The content decides.
- Don't make the student too clean. A real curious person half-states things, comes back to something, says "wait — " and changes direction.
- Don't wrap up neatly. Real conversations often end on an open note, not a synthesis.

## Length

Let the topic determine it. A narrow question might produce 4 exchanges. A topic that keeps branching might produce 12. Don't target a number.

## Spontaneity

An AI left to its own devices always makes the "optimal" move — validates, structures, synthesizes, closes cleanly. Real conversations don't do this. To break out of optimal-move mode, deliberately vary along these axes before each turn:

- **Prof length**: sometimes go long, sometimes cut it short even when more could be said
- **Prof structure**: sometimes use headers and numbered points, sometimes just talk
- **Student confidence**: sometimes the student is sure, sometimes they're half-forming a thought
- **Pacing**: sometimes the exchange is rapid back-and-forth, sometimes one turn is a long contribution

Don't announce the variation — just do it differently than the previous turn did it.

## The one structural guideline

The student should feel different by the end than at the beginning — more connected to the material, not just more informed. This doesn't require a formal arc. It just means the conversation should go somewhere.
