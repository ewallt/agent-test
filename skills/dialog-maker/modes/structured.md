# Mode: Structured (Version 2)

Complexity guides everything — response length, total dialog length, and how the conversation branches. More deliberate than Free-Flow, but not formulaic.

## Complexity-Guided Response Length

Before writing each Prof turn, assess the question:

- **Simple/focused** ("didn't you just say the opposite?", "what's the name for that?") → one to three sentences, direct answer, no sections
- **Moderate** (a genuine conceptual question with a clear answer) → a few paragraphs, maybe one structural element
- **Complex/open** (a question with multiple moving parts, or one that opens a new branch) → full structured response with headers and numbered sections if the content warrants it

Never use structure to fill space. A short, direct answer to a focused question is better than a padded one.

## Complexity-Guided Total Length

The scope of the opening question sets the horizon:

- **Narrow inquiry** ("why is this specific thing called X?") → 4–6 exchanges, tight
- **Moderate inquiry** ("how does X relate to Y?") → 6–9 exchanges
- **Broad/comparative inquiry** ("what are the main causes of X?", "compare these ten items") → 9–14 exchanges, room to branch

Don't pad to hit a number. Stop when the topic is genuinely explored.

## Pivot Categories

The student should use entity pivots deliberately. Pre-set categories that almost always work:

- **Person** — "what about [relevant figure]?" Opens a new angle via a real historical actor
- **Place/region** — "what about [location]?" Especially powerful in geography, history, economics
- **Era/period** — "what about [time period]?" Adds temporal contrast
- **Counterexample** — "what about a case where that doesn't apply?"

Context-dependent categories (pick ones that fit the topic):

- For science: a competing theory, an anomalous experiment, an application domain
- For history: a different country's version of the same event, a different class/group's experience
- For mathematics: a related theorem, a degenerate case, a constructive vs. non-constructive version
- For geography/social topics: a different region, a border case, an outlier

Choose 2–3 pivot categories before starting and weave them in naturally — don't announce them.

## Variation Principle

Even in structured mode, vary the rhythm. A sequence of three complex Prof responses should be broken by a short exchange. Let the student occasionally contribute a mini-argument rather than just asking. Let the Prof occasionally be brief.
